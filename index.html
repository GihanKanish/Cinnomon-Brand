Craft

A skill unchanged
in a hundred years.

The cinnamon quill is one of the few spice products still made entirely by hand. No machines — just a peeling knife, a brass rod, and a process that takes years to master.

01 — HARVEST
Bark harvest

Mature cinnamon shoots — two to three years old — are cut twice a year, timed to the monsoon when sap pressure makes the bark easiest to separate cleanly from the wood.

02 — PREP
Outer scraping

A curved brass rod removes the rough outer bark. Only the pale inner quill — paper-thin and intensely fragrant — is kept. The outer bark goes back into the grove as compost.

03 — ROLL
Quill rolling

While still pliable, strips are nested and rolled into tight cylinders. The diameter of the final roll determines the grade — and grade determines price. This is where the craft is.

04 — DRY
Slow drying

Rolled quills dry in the shade for three to five days — never direct sun, which strips the aromatic oils. This is when the quill locks shape and the cinnamon smell fully develops.
